### 1.2 What each member proves, and why there are three and not one

**`every_civil_date.npk` is the strong one, and it is worth saying exactly how
strong.** Its dates are generated by the calendar — every year, every month,
and every day `days_in_month` admits — never by `days_to_date`. For each it
asserts: `civil_date` accepts it; its day number is `NTIME_DAY_MIN` for the
first date and the previous number plus one for every other; `days_to_date` of
that number is the same date; and the last number is `NTIME_DAY_MAX`. Together
with the harness's count, that makes `date_to_days` an order-preserving
bijection from the calendar's dates onto `[NTIME_DAY_MIN, NTIME_DAY_MAX]`, and
`days_to_date` its inverse — **both functions exactly right on the whole range,
relative to (i) the leap rule and the month table and (ii) the first day's
number.** (i) is `tests/unit/leap_rule.npk`'s, and this subcycle's month-length
member checks it against Hinnant; (ii) is TM-161's — −4 371 587, established
by Python's ordinal and by counting 400-year cycles, neither of them Hinnant,
and asserted since by `range_constants.npk`, `probe07` and the 26 vectors.

**`every_day_number.npk` is implied by it, and is kept for two reasons.** C-16
names both directions as the gate. And its enumeration is independent: it walks
the integers `NTIME_DAY_MIN..NTIME_DAY_MAX` and rests on the two constants
alone, where the civil walk rests on the leap rule and on the test's own nested
loops — each covers the other's way of enumerating wrongly. **What it cannot
see is measured, §5:** a validator that accepts too much. With the century rule
dropped or April given 31 days it exits 0 with the full count, because
Hinnant's `civil_from_days` never produces 1900-02-29 or an April 31st.

**`every_month_length.npk` is implied by the civil walk too** — a month whose
length disagrees with Hinnant breaks the `+ 1` chain at its end — and is kept
because C-17 names it, `TESTING.md` V-2 gives it its own domain (239 988, which
V-16 says a member declares), and **only in a program of its own can it be seen
to fail**: inside the civil walk the chain fires first on every mutant that
breaks a month (PD-17). It compares two implementations of the Gregorian month
that share no code — the table with C-3's leap rule, and Hinnant's
`(153 * mp + 2) / 5` over `yoe / 4 - yoe / 100` eras — and it never calls
`days_to_date`, which the matrix shows (M4, M5, M6: full count).

**What none of them proves:** agreement with an external calendar beyond the 26
vectors (`day_number_vectors.npk`; cycle 0.1.4's cross-oracle, years 1 … 9999,
C-18); anything about a weekday (0.1.3); anything about `Timestamp` (0.2); any
memory property (nothing in the loops allocates — §6).

### 1.3 The weekday cycle is 0.1.3's, and why (PD-16)

**Found at planning.** C-17 (2) is *"the weekday advances by exactly one,
modulo seven, per day"* — a property of a **weekday function**, and `src/cal/`
has none: `weekday_number` maps a `Weekday` to a number, and the weekday OF A
DATE is the cycle README's 0.1.3 checklist, first item. The original plan put
the rider one subcycle before its subject.

**What 0.1.2 can assert about it, it does.** C-13 derives the weekday from the
day number, and a weekday so derived advances by one per day **exactly when
consecutive dates have consecutive day numbers** — which is the civil walk's
`+ 1` chain, asserted at every one of the range's 7 304 483 steps, every
century and every 400-year boundary among them.

**And the rider as written would have been the wrong check.** Measured at
planning, in Python over the whole range: a weekday computed with C's
truncating `%` and no correction — `(n + 3) % 7` in this language, which is
negative on six days in every seven before 1969-12-29 — passes
*"(next − this + 7) % 7 == 1"* on every one of the 7 304 483 steps, and fails a
comparison with an independent count on the second day. So when the rider is written, it compares against a
count begun at −9999-01-01's Monday, and that mutant is its control (§9).

*Declined, with PD-16:* pulling `weekday()` into 0.1.2 — it would design one
derived field apart from the three it is designed with, and take a public name
(TM-013) a subcycle early; a weekday computed in the test from the day number —
implied by the chain everywhere, so one method and not two, and a check of test
code rather than library code; dropping the rider — C-17 names it.

## 2. The domains, recomputed three ways and diffed against every statement of them

**0.1.1's first lesson, applied before a file is written:** a test's asserted
value and a specification's stated value are two lists nobody diffs —
`NTIME_DAY_MIN` was wrong for three weeks beside a probe that had the right
number (TM-161). So every number this subcycle rests on is recomputed by three
methods that share no code with Hinnant's formula or with each other, and then
compared, mechanically, with **every place the tree states it**. And its third
lesson: a second method that equals the first on part of its domain is one
method there — so the ordinal method's 400-year shift is **never zero**.

- **L — the leap rule, summed year by year.** C-3 in Python's arithmetic,
  where a remainder compared with zero has no sign. No `datetime`, no Hinnant.
- **O — Python's own proleptic-Gregorian ordinal** (`date.toordinal()`, the
  currency row), reached through a shift of whole 400-year cycles of 146 097
  days that is forced non-zero: `k = 25, 26` for −9999-01-01 (years 1 and 401),
  `k = −1, −2` for 9999-12-31 (years 9599 and 9199).
- **F — closed form:** 365 a year plus one per leap year, the leap years
  counted as multiples of 4, less multiples of 100, plus multiples of 400.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""0.1.2's expected domains (`0.1.2.md` §2), recomputed three ways that share
no code with Hinnant's formula or with each other, and then DIFFED against
every place the tree states them -- the specifications, the named constants,
the cycle README and the sweep headers. TM-161's lesson: a test's value and a
specification's value are two lists nobody diffs. Exit 0 only if the three
methods agree and every stated value equals them."""
import datetime, glob, os, re, sys

REPO = os.environ["REPO"]
LO, HI = -9999, 9999          # C-4's years, typed from the rule's own text


def leap(y):                  # C-3. Python's `%` floors, and a remainder
    return y % 4 == 0 and (y % 100 != 0 or y % 400 == 0)   # compared with 0 has no sign


def year_days(y):
    return 366 if leap(y) else 365


# L -- the leap rule, summed year by year. No `datetime`, no Hinnant.
L = {"days": sum(year_days(y) for y in range(LO, HI + 1)),
     "day_min": -sum(year_days(y) for y in range(LO, 1970)),
     "day_max": sum(year_days(y) for y in range(1970, HI + 1)) - 1,
     "months": sum(12 for _ in range(LO, HI + 1)),
     "year_min": LO, "year_max": HI}

# O -- Python's own proleptic-Gregorian ordinal, reached through a shift of
# whole 400-year cycles (146 097 days) that is NEVER zero: a zero shift would
# make this the unshifted ordinal on part of its domain, one method there.
E = datetime.date(1970, 1, 1).toordinal()


def shifted(y, m, d, k):
    assert k != 0
    return datetime.date(y + 400 * k, m, d).toordinal() - E - k * 146097


O = {"day_min": {k: shifted(LO, 1, 1, k) for k in (25, 26)},      # years 1, 401
     "day_max": {k: shifted(HI, 12, 31, k) for k in (-1, -2)}}     # 9599, 9199


# F -- closed form: 365 a year plus one per leap year, the leap years counted
# as multiples of 4, less multiples of 100, plus multiples of 400.
def multiples(k):
    return HI // k - (LO - 1) // k


F = {"leaps": multiples(4) - multiples(100) + multiples(400),
     "months": (HI - LO + 1) * 12}
F["days"] = (HI - LO + 1) * 365 + F["leaps"]

agree = (L["days"] == F["days"]
         and all(v == L["day_min"] for v in O["day_min"].values())
         and all(v == L["day_max"] for v in O["day_max"].values())
         and L["day_max"] - L["day_min"] + 1 == L["days"]
         and L["months"] == F["months"])
print("L: days=%d day_min=%d day_max=%d months=%d" % (
    L["days"], L["day_min"], L["day_max"], L["months"]))
print("O: day_min %s  day_max %s" % (O["day_min"], O["day_max"]))
print("F: leap years=%d days=%d months=%d" % (F["leaps"], F["days"],
                                               F["months"]))
print("the three methods %s" % ("AGREE" if agree else "DISAGREE"))


def num(s):                   # "−4 371 587", "+2 932 896", "7 304 484"
    return int(s.replace("−", "-").replace("+", "").replace(" ", ""))


N = r"([−+-]?\d[\d ]*\d|\d)"
STATED = [
    ("meta/specs/CALENDAR.md", r"\| minimum day number \(epoch 1970-01-01 = 0\) \| `" + N + "`", "day_min"),
    ("meta/specs/CALENDAR.md", r"\| maximum day number \| `" + N + "`", "day_max"),
    ("meta/specs/CALENDAR.md", r"\| \*\*total days in range\*\* \| \*\*" + N + r"\*\*", "days"),
    ("meta/specs/CALENDAR.md", r"Every day number in `\[" + N + ", ", "day_min"),
    ("meta/specs/CALENDAR.md", r"Every day number in `\[[^,]+, " + N + r"\]`", "day_max"),
    ("meta/specs/CALENDAR.md", r"That is " + N + " cases in each direction", "days"),
    ("meta/specs/TESTING.md", r"\| " + N + r" × 2 \| 0\.1 \|", "days"),
    ("meta/specs/TESTING.md", r"every \(year, month\) in range \| " + N + r" \|", "months"),
    ("meta/specs/CALENDAR.md", r"`year ∈ \[" + N + ", ", "year_min"),
    ("meta/specs/CALENDAR.md", r"`year ∈ \[[^,]+, " + N + r"\]`", "year_max"),
    ("src/core/limits.npk", r"NTIME_YEAR_MIN = (-?\d+)i64;", "year_min"),
    ("src/core/limits.npk", r"NTIME_YEAR_MAX = (-?\d+)i64;", "year_max"),
    ("src/core/limits.npk", r"NTIME_DAY_MIN = (-?\d+)i64;", "day_min"),
    ("src/core/limits.npk", r"NTIME_DAY_MAX = (-?\d+)i64;", "day_max"),
    ("meta/roadmap/0.1/README.md", r"every day number in `\[" + N + ", ", "day_min"),
    ("meta/roadmap/0.1/README.md", r"every day number in `\[[^,]+, " + N + r"\]`", "day_max"),
    ("meta/roadmap/0.1/README.md", r"== n` — " + N + " cases", "days"),
    ("meta/roadmap/0.1/README.md", r"== d` — " + N + " cases", "days"),
    ("meta/roadmap/0.1/README.md", r"every \(year, month\) in range — " + N + " cases", "months"),
]
DOMAIN_OF = {"every_day_number": "days", "every_civil_date": "days",
             "every_month_length": "months"}
headers = sorted(glob.glob(os.path.join(REPO, "tests/unit/sweep/*.npk")))
for path in headers:
    stem = os.path.basename(path)[:-4]
    STATED.append((os.path.relpath(path, REPO),
                   r"^// sweep-count: (\d+)$", DOMAIN_OF.get(stem, "?" + stem)))

bad = 0
for rel, pattern, what in STATED:
    with open(os.path.join(REPO, rel), encoding="utf-8") as fh:
        text = fh.read()
    # A tag in an HTML comment (`TESTING.md` V-1h) sits beside the number it

      "%d disagreement(s)" % (len(STATED), len({r for r, _, _ in STATED}),
                              len(headers), bad))
```

## 10. What will bite

- **`earlyoom` is live and has fired today** (the board's hazard 12). A step
  that dies by SIGTERM (exit 143) or SIGKILL (137) may be the machine: read
  `journalctl -u earlyoom --since today` **before** diagnosing anything, and
  treat a kill as W-12's red — a stop, never a retry, and never a defect report
  until the journal is read. Nothing in this subcycle is large (§6), so a kill
  here is almost certainly another workload's.
- **A tag written whole in `meta/roadmap/` is a claim** `check_denominators`
  will hold the tree to, the moment the file is saved — including in the
  execution record. Quote a tag's name and value, never the bracketed form;
  `tags.py` assembles the real ones.
- **Mutant labels are `M0` … `M13`, with no hyphen.** `check_specs_current`
  reads `M-13` as `TIME_MODEL.md`'s rule, and `N-`, `F-`, `S-`, `B-`, `C-`,
  `V-`, `Z-` the same way.
- **The marker block is contiguous from line 1** (TM-121): each file's two
  markers, then prose. A marker-shaped line below the block — `//`, one space, a
  known key, a colon, at column 0 — fails the run. The headers mention
  `sweep-count` only mid-line.
- **The count is incremented AFTER the checks**, so it counts verified cases.
  Moved before them, a sweep that failed on its last case would print the full
  count and exit non-zero — still red, but a worse message.
- **`..` is inclusive; `...` is exclusive**, and an empty range runs zero times
  rather than counting down (measured). `loop`/`till` are not used: a literal
  step arms `BadStep` (DEF-95, spurious), and a `for` needs no arm at all.
- **`raw` calls a `never fails` function; a fallible one returns a `Result`**
  whose `.value` may be read only after `.is_error` (TM-162).
- **`date_to_days` is total**, so a date that should not exist still gets a
  number — the next day's. Any new rider that builds a date with the value
  under test and then counts to it will agree with a wrong value (§5 reading 5).
- **Do not "fix" §2's second method to shift by zero** where it could: a zero
  shift makes it the plain ordinal there, and then it is one method, not two.
