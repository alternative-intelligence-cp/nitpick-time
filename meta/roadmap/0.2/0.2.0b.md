## 1. What was measured — the facts this plan rests on

All at compiler `5fbaf4a`, over `db05d8d`'s tree; blocks 0a and 0b re-derive each.

### 1.1 The starting tree

0.2.0a's record, `db05d8d`: `GREEN -- 113 unit(s), 0 failures; 5 pending`; 128 `.npk`, 91 of which compile as a root
with no diagnostic; decisions through TM-213. `src/core/vec.npk` declares `pub struct:Vec<T>` and nine generic
verbs, `vec_at` alone bounded, `T: Copy` since 0.2.0a (TM-211).

### 1.2 The bound on the type, and why every verb must say it

`pub struct:Vec<T: Copy>` compiles; with it and nothing else, each of the eight verbs that does not state the bound
is `NITPICK-TYPE-017` at its own definition — *"`T` does not implement `Copy`, which `Vec`'s parameter `T`
requires"* — `vec_init`, `vec_reserve`, `vec_push`, `vec_set`, `vec_pop`, `vec_truncate`, `vec_clear` and
`vec_free`. The compiler's `TRAITS_REFERENCE.md` §3.2 says why: a generic body is checked once, against the bounds
it declares. With all nine bounded, `vec.npk` compiles.

### 1.3 What it changes: no code, and three verdicts

With every verb bounded — `bound`, which moves no line — all 128 tracked `.npk` compiled in `db05d8d`'s tree and in
the bounded copy (block 0b's `moved` and `irsame`): **125 verdicts unchanged, and the 89 files that compile in both
emit byte-identical IR, no mask needed.** The three that move:

- `tests/unit/vec_churn_pop.npk` and `vec_churn_clear.npk` (TM-150's pair, committed by TM-196), from compiling to
  `NITPICK-TYPE-017` at five sites each — the `Vec<string>` declaration, its turbofish, and each verb's call;
- `tests/probe/defect/generic_owning_copy/case5_vec_at_destructive.npk`, from two sites (its two `vec_at` calls)
  to six — its declaration, its turbofish, `vec_push`, the two calls and `vec_free`.

`nitpick-regex`'s planner measured exactly these three in a scratch clone at `db05d8d` (its 0.1.1b §6); this plan
measured them again rather than carry them (the workbench PLAYBOOK §12: a list of consequences is a hypothesis).

### 1.4 Every element that owns, and every consumer shape

As the element, `string`, `Bytes`, `Vec<int64>`, `cstring`, a pointer `int64->`, a slice `uint8[]`, an optional
`int64?` and a fixed array `int64[4]` are each `NITPICK-TYPE-017` at the type, the turbofish and the verb (block 0b).
A consumer's struct field `Vec<string>`, a generic wrapper's unbounded `Vec<U>` and a parameter `Vec<cstring>->` are
refused where each is written. A struct of scalars that does not claim `Copy` is refused the same way, so **an
element type derives `Copy`** — `Instant` at 0.2.0 and `FmtPart` at 0.4 (§8).

### 1.5 `Copy` is this library's whole element rule — and is not `nitpick-regex`'s

`Copy` is the compiler's "owns nothing, so drops nothing" (D-327). A `#[derive(Copy)]` struct holding a pointer
`int64->` or a slice `uint8[]` is a legal element — measured, both compile — and one holding a `cstring` is
`NITPICK-DERIVE-006` (D-328 made `cstring` own). `nitpick-regex`'s element rule asks an element to drop nothing AND
to hold no block of its own, so it kept `check_vec_elements_own_nothing` for the second half (its RX-190). **This
library's rule is S-18d's: the four verbs that discard an element owe it a drop, so an element must have none to
owe** — which `Copy` states whole. A pointer or a slice in an element is a VIEW, and S-18e (a view's lifetime) and
S-22 (a view is never returned) govern a view however it is held; `src/` declares no `Vec` of anything yet (the first
is cycle 0.4's `Vec<FmtPart>`), so a check would read nothing. PD-63 declines one, and §8 hands the one question it
leaves to the check that will meet it.

### 1.6 The churn pair, and why it retires rather than moves

The pair measured what `vec_pop` and `vec_clear` do at `T = string` — 120 bytes at the peak against 48 000 096 over
two million cycles — the evidence TM-150 gave for `vec_pop` owing no drop. Under the bound that `T` cannot be
written, so the pair measures a type that no longer ships. Re-homed in `tests/probe/` over a `Vec` of its own, it
would be a third copy of the leak-and-remedy pairs `probe06b`/`probe06c` and `probe12`/`probe12b` already are — and
TM-196 put it in `tests/unit/` precisely because it asserted this library's own verbs. **The instrument keeps two
known leaks and two remedies**: `heap_bounded` 8 → 6 and `cap_belted` 6 → 4, `TESTING.md` V-17's three twin pairs
two again. The pair's text stays at `db05d8d`, and its numbers in TM-150.

### 1.7 What else the counts move, and one count already stale

Two files fewer: `npk_total` 128 → 126, `npk_tests` 118 → 116, `suite_roots` 78 → 76, the unit suite 22 → 20 and a
full run **113 → 111 units**; the parse split `91 + 35 + 2` → `89 + 35 + 2`. **Found while moving them**:
`harness/run.py`'s `run_parse` restates its tagged sentence as an untagged equation, which read `127 = 5 + 77 + 3 + 1
